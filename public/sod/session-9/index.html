Part One

The Dating
Challenge.

The mission

Two goals. One weekly rhythm.

1

Get your numbers up

More real interactions with real people. Volume changes everything.

2

Practice dating

Dating is a skill. Skills need reps, not theory.

→

Weekly: meet 3 new people

Enough interaction that you would want to go out with them, and they know enough about you to want to go out with you.

Here is the catch

You don't have to go out with any of them.

You don't even have to be that interested. It's not about values yet. If a date happens, great. If it doesn't, it is still a win.

Underneath the practical goals

The heart goals.

1

Connect with yourself

Notice what comes up in your body and your thoughts as you meet people. Be specific.

2

Build confidence

Find ease in talking with the other gender at an emotional level, not just a work or intellectual one.

3

Lower the stakes

Rewrite the script that takes dating SO seriously. It can feel like a scavenger hunt. You can do this.

Why this works

We're working a muscle.

  • Many of us connect with the opposite gender just fine at work or in the group chat.
  • Emotional connection is a different muscle, and it's the one dating actually uses.
  • As you go, analyze what's happening inside you. What's in your body? What are your thoughts doing?

Accountability

Every Monday, share your 3 people with the group.

Who you met, how you met them, and what you learned about yourself. Track it in the Dating Journal at the back of your workbook.

Where do I even meet people?

8 ways to meet people.

1

Your normal life

Coffee shops, the gym, the grocery store, the dog park. Just be more open, present, and aware.

2

Pick up a hobby

Dancing, a book club, a league, that thing you've always felt the nudge for.

3

At church

Or another church nearby. Go to other churches' events. We're one body of Christ.

4

Go to events

Accept invitations where you don't know everyone. Conferences, Meetup.com, professional events.

5

Serve or volunteer

Great people are serving. Church, food pantry, nonprofits. The skies are endless.

6

Ask for a setup

Ask a few trusted friends. Stay open. Don't hand them your entire list. Just go for it.

7

Online

Tonight's Part Two. Cast the wide net.

8

Social media

It counts. Real connection starts in a lot of places now.

Activity

Make your list.

From the 8 ways, write a list of the places around you where you could meet people in the next month.

Part Two

Online
dating.

Scale of 1 to 10: how has online dating gone for you? 10 is amazing. 1 is "I deleted it last night for the 7th time."

Remember the mindset reset

Scarcity sees scarcity.

And when you only see scarcity, you miss the abundance that is always available to you.

  • Disappointment is guaranteed. Ghosting is guaranteed.
  • But you get to choose how you respond. Not react. Respond.
  • Chat: are you open to seeing online dating in a new way?
Let's debunk them

The online dating myths.

"It's not biblical"→God works through people meeting. The medium isn't the message.
"It doesn't work"→It hasn't worked for you, so far. Often the expectations were the problem.
"It takes too much time"→10 minutes in the morning, 10 at night. You make time for what matters.
"It's desperate"→Is going to a job fair desperate? It's just showing up where the people are.
"It's hookups only"→Yes and no. Your profile and your clarity filter for you.
The expectations reset

Four reminders.

1

Cast a wide net

And embrace that God can work through online dating.

2

Reduce the pressure

Trust the process. Have fun. Hold it loosely.

3

Lowest stakes in dating

A stranger on the internet not replying back to you after a few days should not break your heart.

4

80% won't convert

Most matches never become first dates or quality dating situations. That's normal, not failure.

Part Three

Set up your
profile.

Grab your Online Dating Bonus Guide: mindset, app, setup, method.

Where to be

The apps we recommend.

Hinge ARK Dating Bumble Holy Salt Coffee Meets Bagel Christian Mingle Upward ChristianCafe

Not every app is great for your location. All have solid free offerings. Start unpaid, with two apps at a time.

Photos

What not to post.

Retire these

  • Photos with the opposite gender (especially exes)
  • Distant, blurry, or old photos (2+ years)
  • Group photos (one max)
  • Work or LinkedIn photos
  • Every photo in sunglasses or a hat

Also these

  • Cropped photos and emoji-covered faces. Are you Batman?
  • Bathroom, gym, or car selfies
  • Heavy filters and intense expressions
  • No babies. No fish. No dead animals.
  • No bathing suits
Photos

What to post.

The formula

  • First photo: half-body, smiling, great light, great angle
  • Photos doing what you love
  • Variety: close-up, action, travel, dressier, everyday
  • At least one full-body shot

The standard

  • Clear and crisp, natural light
  • A dressier one (think wedding, cocktail hour)
  • Photos that show your personality AND your values
  • The right person will love all of you. Show up as you.
Your bio

Three rules.

1

Captivate curiosity

Give them a reason to reach out. Ask a question they can answer. Two truths and a lie is fun. NO generic comma lists.

2

Be clear on what you want

You're looking for a Christian and a relationship. You are not looking for a pen pal.

3

Keep it playful

Show personality. Overrated takes, fun hills to die on. This is not a job interview.

The golden rule

Get off the app. Five messages to meet.

48 hours to a few days. The app is the introduction, not the relationship.

Know the signs

The texting pen pal.

  • Never initiates an in-person meetup or a phone call.
  • Promises a first date but never follows up. Hot and cold, but always comes back around.
  • Texts you their life story and starts vulnerable conversations without ever meeting.
  • Makes you feel special, then goes silent for days.
  • Questions you if you don't respond fast enough.
  • You constantly doubt the connection during your time apart.
For the ladies · moving to a call or date

The open, honest script.

Word for word

"Hey, it feels so encouraging to connect with someone I feel so good talking to here online. Yet for me, I am not someone who likes texting a lot. A phone call would feel so great for me. What do you think?"

Not comfortable sharing your number yet? Suggest a Zoom. And if he agrees: "What time or day were you thinking?" If he never follows through, name it kindly, and hold your standard of follow-through.

For the men · reaching out

Four rules for the first message.

1

~90 characters

Short and sweet beats a novel. Every time.

2

Be specific

Name something real from her profile that stood out to you.

3

Be curious

"You mention…" "Good taste." "Curious what…"

4

Watch your grammar

Bad spelling is a huge turn-off. "haha" and "lol" in moderation are fine.

For the men · from the app to the date

Call it a date.

Word for word

"Hey, I've really enjoyed connecting with you. I'd love to get to know you better. Would you be open to going out on a date with me sometime? :)"

Not a hangout. A date. Then suggest a day and time right away: "Are you free Tuesday at 6?" Follow up with the plan so she can prepare. A phone or Zoom call first is a great option that helps her feel safe.
