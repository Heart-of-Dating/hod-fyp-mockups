Red flags, dealbreakers
& non-negotiables.

Tonight we get super tactical.

Hear this clearly, first

Pain Cycle work does not go on these lists.

  • Your triggers don't become dealbreakers.
  • Your coping mechanisms don't become a wishlist for what they should manage.
  • Your pain message doesn't become a job description for a future partner.

Pain Cycle work stays your work — regardless of who they are or how they show up.

So why bring it up at all?

Pain Cycle gives you the eyes to build clean lists.

It helps you separate "this activates my wound" from "this is who they are."

  • Without that separation, your dealbreaker list grows to 30 items.
  • Your red flag list scares you out of every relationship.
  • Your non-negotiables become "a partner who manages me."
  • Short lists. That describe them — not your relationship to them.
Part One · Dealbreakers

What is a dealbreaker?

"Things I will not tolerate."

A circumstance or behavior you know you don't want in a long-term relationship.

It should be based on your values.

They're unique to each person — but there's a rule of thumb worth going by.

The rule of thumb

Our top three.

1

Spiritual incompatibility

Not in a committed, growing relationship with Christ. If you have to guess, it's not it.

2

Moral & safety

Any form of abuse — emotional, verbal, physical. Active addiction or major habitual sin.

3

Vision misalignment

Your visions for life don't line up. They don't want kids — and you do.

Check your list

What isn't a dealbreaker.

Some things end up on the list that shouldn't necessarily be there.

  • Politics — values matter; party affiliation often doesn't.
  • Location — distance can be solved if everything else fits.
  • Height.
  • Debt — depends on why, and what they're doing about it.
  • Past relationships, been married before — the pattern matters, not the resume.
Activity

Build your dealbreakers list.

Three to five. No more.

Too long = nothing's actually a dealbreaker.

The test for each one: would a fully healed, fully secure person also call this a dealbreaker? If only you would, because it touches your wound, that's pain cycle homework, not a partner filter.

Part Two · Red flags

What is a red flag?

A sign to pause and investigate — not a reason to run. And the reality is, we all have them to some extent.

  • Behaviors worth investigating that might reveal character issues.
  • A sign to pause, ask questions, and get more information.
  • Not automatically a reason to walk away.
Two big mistakes

Most people do one of these.

Eliminate instantly

See one red flag at the start and cut the person off. Big mistake.

Ignore entirely

Pretend the flags aren't there because you like them. Also a big mistake.

We're going to talk about both.

How red turns to orange, then green

The three-step protocol.

1

Ask questions

Get curious. It takes courage. Without the conversation you're just assuming — and the conversation itself can bring you closer.

2

Watch how they respond

Defensive? Don't see a problem? Or do they already have awareness and are already working on it?

3

Observe through time

Does it actually change? You want them to change because they want to — not to win you over. Otherwise it breeds resentment.

The litmus question: would they change this even if you were not in their life?

Worth investigating

Some major red flags.

  • Pride — a lack of humility.
  • Doesn't have godly friendships.
  • Mainly friends with the opposite gender — flirty friendships.
  • Doesn't have boundaries, or respect them — emotional, spiritual, physical.
  • Dismissive of growth.
Activity

Build your red flags list.

Three to five.

Too long = you're scanning for fear, not discernment.

Part Three · What you're looking for

Non-negotiables.

"Things I am intentionally looking for."

Dealbreakers protect you from the wrong person.

Non-negotiables help you recognize the right one.

Definition

What is a non-negotiable?

Character-based, not preference-based. The test: "Would this quality bring me closer to God, and line up with the values I have for my life?"

  • The core qualities you're intentionally looking for in a partner.
  • They reflect what matters most in your life and future.
  • They help you recognize the right person — not just avoid the wrong one.
What they are not

Preferences aren't foundations.

  • Physical traits — height, looks, style.
  • Status traits — money, job title, popularity.
  • Hyper-specific preferences — "must love X hobby."
How many?

Five or less.

  • Too many = an unrealistic checklist.
  • Too few = a lack of clarity.
Prompt one · from your pain message

What character makes the lie harder to believe?

"I am unloved"→Faithful · Consistent
"I am unsafe"→Integrity · Trustworthy
"I am forgotten / unseen"→Attentive · Present
"I am unworthy"→Honoring · Humble
"I am out of control"→Steady · Self-led

Every one of these is a character trait. Faithful at work. Steady with roommates. Integrity with their boss. The trait is them — not what they do for you.

Prompt two · from your coping mechanism

What character gives your growth work room to breathe?

Control→Grounded · TrustingA person who trusts God doesn't need to control outcomes
Escape→Patient · SteadfastA person who doesn't run when things get hard
Shame→Encouraging · HonestHonesty that doesn't punish your sensitivity
Blame→Humble · TeachableModeling the ownership you're growing in

Not to manage your coping — you're asking that their character not make your growth work harder.

Prompt three · from your core values

Translate your top three values into character.

If you value "growth" → their trait is "teachable."

Watch your personality type
  • Hopeless Romantic / Safety Seeker — throwing out the list for a person.
  • Lone Wolf / Overthinker / Intentional — way too many things on the bill that no one can measure up to.
The difference

Weak vs. strong.

Weak — preference-based

  • "Tall"
  • "Funny"
  • "Successful"
  • "Musician"

Strong — character-based

  • Growing relationship with God
  • Emotionally healthy · self-aware
  • Growth-minded · teachable
  • Honoring
  • Communicates honestly

Non-negotiables aren't about finding a perfect person — they're about identifying the right foundation.

Activity

Establish the list.

Your five non-negotiable qualities.

Should be five. Character, not preference. Run each through the prompts.

Put it all together

The final filter framework.

1Are there any dealbreakers?If yes → stop
2Are there red flags?Get curious
3Do they have my non-negotiables?If no → don't force it
