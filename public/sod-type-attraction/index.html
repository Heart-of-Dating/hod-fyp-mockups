Part One

What is
type?

Everything we've learned about our parts of self and attachment wounds goes hand in hand with this.

Activity · Workbook pg 57

"They're not my type."

Ever said it? Picture your "type" in your head.

Write out what they are like —
include as many details as possible.

Where type comes from

Type can come from childhood wounding.

Deficits you didn't get from your parents or caretakers.

You're drawn — subconsciously — to something you believe will medicate your pain.

It's one of the biggest ways we medicate later in life from the wounds we carried as a child.

Part Two

Preferences vs.
non-negotiables.

Hold them loosely

A preference is not a deal breaker.

You can absolutely have preferences. But are your preferences quietly becoming non-negotiables?

  • A preference should mean exactly that — a preference.
  • Preferences dictated by wounding or lack lead to disappointment.
  • Honest question: has your type actually worked out for you?
  • Or has it left you more disappointed and heartbroken?

No human on earth can save you from your pain or complete you.

And a side note — type isn't just preferences. Type is, and should be, made up of their character, spirit, and soul.

Why we keep going back

We're addicted to the very thing that wounds us.

Why? Because it's familiar.

Logically, you know it isn't working. But the neural pathways are worn in — even the neglect feels like home.

If you hand another person the things you need to be okay — you hand them all the control.

It's healthiest to hold your preferences loosely.

Part Three

Dating the
wrong people.

"Why do the people I want never want me — and the people I don't want always like me?"

What's happening · one of two things

Unavailable feels alluring.

With attachment wounds, we're drawn to what we can't have. It works in marketing — and it works in dating.

  • Unattainable reads as more worthy, more valuable.
  • Mystery makes us fill in the blanks.
  • "They're probably so godly."
  • "Such a leader. Such an amazing partner."
  • …just because they're unavailable.
Where it started

If a parent was unavailable…

…you grew accustomed to that lack of attention. You dreamed of earning it. And now you're drawn to the same pattern — wanting unavailable people to love you.

What's happening · the second thing

The halo effect.

  • They match the fantasy of our type…
  • …so we assign a perfect life behind their appearance.
  • We chase "out of our league" to feel more worthy, more loved, more enough.
  • Then we perform. Market ourselves. Sell ourselves. No mistakes allowed.
  • And when we don't get them — it "proves" every negative thing we believe about ourselves.
so godly… such a leader… amazing partner…

The perfect life we invented for them

What to do instead · one · Activity

Rethink your yes.

Picture your last crush — someone you asked out, dated, or simply fantasized about.

Why were you truly interested in them?
Was your why based on fantasy — or reality?

Did you overlook, or fill in the blanks about, their character?

What to do instead · two · Activity

Rethink your no.

A "no" isn't only when someone asks you out. You silently reject people all the time — on the app, at church, at an event.

Picture someone you've rejected — verbally, or just internally. Why were you not interested? What's the quality of your qualifications?

Is there a chance you can challenge yourself here?

1

Emotional
attraction.

Vulnerability. Emotional safety.

And remember — emotionally intelligent is not the same as emotionally healthy.

  • Do you feel seen?
  • Do you feel known?
  • Can you share your feelings safely?
  • Can you share your past safely?
  • Can you have hard conversations with them?
2

Spiritual
attraction.

Being attracted to the Jesus in them.

46%

of Christians surveyed said spiritual attraction was the most important kind.

Debra Fileta · Love in Every Season

3

Intellectual
attraction.

The desire to connect on a more cerebral level.

  • You want to spend time with them because of what you talk about.
  • They make you think about things in new and challenging ways.
  • For some, intellectual attraction is the doorway to emotional and romantic attraction.
  • Different people fill different roles and needs in our lives.
4

Personality
attraction.

This is the friendship part of the relationship.

  • How they act, behave, communicate, think, socialize — their patterns and passions.
  • And you must understand your own personality in this.
  • Kait: "I used to want the guy who'd talk feelings for hours — until I realized staying stuck in feelings crushed my soul."
5

Physical
attraction.

The first one we see — because it's the easiest.

Some of us have carried a picture of who we'd end up with. Ladies — maybe he was really muscular. Guys — the digital age has sold you unrealistic expectations of women.

  • Sometimes there's a spark. Many times it's a slow flame that takes time.
  • You can become drawn to someone through time.
  • Be realistic — people have real bodies, and bodies change.
  • Beauty is fluid. In the Victorian era, pale skin and major curves were the standard.
