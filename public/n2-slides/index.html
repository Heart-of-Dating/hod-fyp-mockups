The one question

Is this ambiguous
or is this intentional?

And if it's ambiguous — will you actually do something about it?

Part 1 · Name it

The 5 Faces of ambiguous dating.

Everyone here is nodding. Everyone here has a different picture in their head.

01of five

The Endless
Texter.

Three months in. You've never been on a date that was clearly a date. But you're texting all day — and it feels like dating.

If your sister heard it out loud, she'd squint.

02of five

"We're
just talking."

No label. Lots of intimacy. All the weight of a relationship with none of the safety.

Welcome to no man's land.

03of five

The Hot
& Cold.

They initiate → go dark → reappear the moment you've started to move on. Multiple "this is done, I'm OVER it" moments. They know exactly when to text — and what to text.

A.K.A. the MPI — minimal possible investment.

A.K.A. the MPI guy

Watch what minimal possible investment looks like in the wild.

04of five

The Almost.

Everything but the commitment. He says the right things. You meet his friends. Six months in, he's "not ready." Three months later — apparently he was. With someone else.

05of five

"Just
friends."

Worship team. Small group. Wedding party. They've told you you're "like a sister" — and they also text at 11pm.

You are MORE than just friends.
Except you're not.

The unifying diagnosis

You're reading the pulse.
You're missing the pattern.

The part that's gonna sting

You.
Are. Choosing.
It.

There is not one of these 5 faces that you do not have the authority and the agency to clarify. Not one.

We love to make it someone else's fault
"He's keeping me in limbo."
"She won't define this."
"He gives mixed signals."
"She won't commit."
All true.
AND —
You are allowed to not participate in it.

With your full permission.
Part 2 · The why

Three reasons
you've stayed.

You hate ambiguity. You complain every weekend. And yet — here you are. Why?

01reason one

Ambiguity feels
safer than asking.

As long as you don't ask — the possibility is alive. Hope is alive. "Maybe" is alive.

You're not staying because you love them. You're staying because you don't want to grieve them.

Closure is scarier than confusion.

You've confused being patient with being passive.
You think you're being chill.
You're being confused — and calling it virtue.

02reason two

You don't feel
allowed to ask.

Patient women don't ask. Godly women wait for the man to lead. Right?

You have the agency.

03reason three

You don't
actually want it.

You think you deserve clarity. You think you're entitled to it. But that puts the responsibility on the other person.

You have to WANT a healthy relationship.

You can't step up and participate in a healthy relationship if you think the other person exists to serve you, read your mind, and initiate toward you.

Part 3 · The cost

Ambiguity isn't
"so what."
It's costing you.

The gym analogy

Six months at the StairMaster.
Macros tracked. Peptides stacked.
Your body looks exactly the same.

That's what ambiguous dating is. Time in. Heart in. Prayer in. Nothing measurable back.

The cost nobody talks about

Every hour obsessing over them is an hour not—

  • Building friendships
  • Investing in your community
  • Pouring into God's Kingdom
  • Pursuing your purpose
  • Going on dates with someone who actually could be the one
  • Having fun
  • Being present to your own life

You only have so many hours. Ambiguity is hoarding them.

Part 4 · The how

Three steps to intentional dating.

In order. You can't skip ahead.

01
Step one · The foundation

Start with a healthy, godly standard of relationship.

If you don't know what marriage you're walking toward — what values, what dealbreakers — you'll run on feelings. And feelings change with the weather.

Value-based decision-making.

The most powerful thing JJ will show you tonight

Watch what changes.

Fear-based

"I'm terrified of getting rejected again. You've been acting kind of funny. You checked my IG story but didn't text back. I saw on Matt's story you were at Bailey's trivia and you didn't text me back so I'm wondering if maybe…"

→ He withdraws. You spiral harder.

Value-based

"Hey — I really value honesty and healthy, consistent communication in a dating relationship. What we've had over the last few weeks hasn't quite been that. I'd love that from you — I want to invite you into it. Where are you at?"

→ Grounded. An invitation, not an accusation.

The first one makes them feel accused. The second one makes them feel invited.

Live role-play

Spot the fear-based
response.

Take 1
vs.
Take 2

Same conversation. Two different versions. Watch which one you recognize.

How they respond IS the answer

Red light. Green light.

That's all you need.

The right person

"Yeah — I've been thinking about that too. I want to keep moving toward something with you. Can we talk about what that looks like for us?"

Clarity. Even a "I'm not there yet" is still clarity — and still a gift.

The wrong person

"Whoa, okay. I wasn't expecting this. I don't know — I guess I haven't really thought about it. Can we just… let things happen?"

Avoidance. They make you feel like the question was the problem. It wasn't.

02
Step two

Time without
movement is your answer.

Intentional dating moves. Ambiguous dating loops. If nothing has moved in 60 days — that lack of movement is the data.

"They're just busy" is not the gift you think it is.

Relationships can still form in a busy season. Don't let them tell you otherwise.

Ask: has anything actually moved in the last 60 days?

03
Step three

Read pattern,
not pulse.

Stop reading interest off a single text. Interest lives in only two places.

  • TIME — consistent presence, or weekend crumbs?
  • FOLLOW-THROUGH — does what they say match what they do?
  • FUTURE-TENSE — do they plan three weeks out, or only this Saturday?
  • INTEGRATION — do you exist in their life, or in a compartment?

Is this based on FACTS — patterns and actions —
or am I writing a STORY about how they feel in this moment?

The three steps

From ambiguous to intentional.

Write this down

I don't have to live in confusion.
I can date with clarity.

You are not "too much" for wanting clarity. You are not "high maintenance" for wanting direction.

And — you have a part to play in healthy relationships. It's not all on them.
